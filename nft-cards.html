WHY NFT CARDS BEAT FLAT JPEGS

Design Scroll · Sep 3, 2026 · 5 min read · by Avrelia

We almost shipped CLAUDIUS as nine flat images in a grid. The night before launch someone posted a trading card game trailer in our group chat, the kind with ornate card backs and a slow flip, and we delayed the launch 5 days to rebuild everything around a question that had somehow never occurred to us: why does nobody film themselves opening a folder of JPEGs?

WHAT A CARD DOES THAT AN IMAGE CAN'T

People film themselves opening card packs, and those videos pull millions of views, because the 3 seconds before the reveal carry the emotion. A card has a hidden state. There's a back that says wait, a front that says now, and a frame that says this is an object. NFT projects copied the rarity math from trading cards and mostly skipped the part that makes a kid beg for one more pack.

WHAT WE CHANGED

Our homepage now deals nine cards face down, all wearing the same gold and charcoal back, then flips them open in a wave. On the collection page each card tilts under your cursor, wears its rarity badge where a grading slab would sit, and carries one line of lore where a trading card prints flavor text. None of it changes the token. It changes quite a lot about how the first visit feels, and first visits are where collectors are made.

IF YOU'RE BUILDING A COLLECTION

Give the artwork a hidden state, because a reveal is a moment and a grid is not. Put a frame and a badge on the card, since framed things read as objects. Write one line of flavor text under the name. And show the rarity tier on the card itself rather than burying it in metadata where only tools can see it.

The rebuild cost us those 5 days. The face-down deal is now the single most commented-on thing about the site, ahead of the art itself, which I have complicated feelings about as the person who drew the art.
