HOW TO SELL PIXEL ART NFTS

Market Scroll · Sep 3, 2026 · 6 min read · by Kaiser

Our first listing sat for 9 days without a single offer. Same art you can see on the collection page today, same price. Then we rewrote one paragraph in the description and it sold in 6 hours. Selling pixel art comes down to 3 decisions, and most creators only ever think about the first one.

WHERE YOU LIST

For 1/1 pixel work on Ethereum, OpenSea is still where collectors filter and browse. The create flow takes about 20 minutes: connect a wallet, create the collection, set the royalty (we use 5%), upload, price. Two fields people skip. Fill in every trait, because traits are how collectors filter their way to you. And write the collection description before you list anything, since it's the first thing a buyer reads and most sellers leave it one sentence long.

THE PIXEL ART PRICE

A 1/1 has no floor price to lean on, so build a band. We looked up what comparable pixel collections had actually sold for, then set tiers: 1.60 ETH for the accessible pieces, up to 4.20 for the deep lore gods. The genesis piece is priced low on purpose, because a cheap entry turns a browser into a holder, and holders come back for a second. One warning from experience: never quietly lower a public price. Collectors screenshot, and the screenshots circulate for months.

THE PARAGRAPH THAT SOLD IT

The change that ended our 9 day drought was small. We deleted the trait list from the top of the description and put the god's story there instead. "Teal body, gold band, 1 of 1" became "It reads the next block before it is written, and never says how." Six hours later the relic was gone. Write the one sentence a holder would repeat at a party, put it first, and let the traits live underneath it.

So, for your own listing: pick the marketplace where your medium's collectors already browse, complete every field before going live, price in tiers with one honest entry point, and open the description with the story sentence. The art stops the scroll. The listing gets the signature.
