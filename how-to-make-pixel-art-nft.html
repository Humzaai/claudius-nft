HOW TO MAKE PIXEL ART NFTS

Forge Scroll · Sep 3, 2026 · 7 min read · by Avrelia

The first god took 11 attempts. Pixel art at 20x20 gives you 400 squares, and every one of them is a decision somebody will later zoom into on a 27 inch monitor. Here's the process we ended up with, including the parts we got wrong.

PICK A GRID ONCE

We use 20x20. CryptoPunks used 24x24. Under 16 the faces stop reading, and above 32 you're drawing small illustrations rather than pixel art. Whatever you pick, keep it for the whole collection. Nine characters at the same resolution is a big part of why they feel related.

FIVE COLORS PER CHARACTER

Body, shade, highlight, outline, one accent. Ours is the gold laurel. A sixth color always looks harmless the day you add it and always shows up in the finished set as drift. A tight palette also lets recolors read as rarity tiers, and collectors treat them that way.

SILHOUETTE BEFORE FACE

Fill the character in solid black first. If a stranger can't tell what it is from the outline, more facial detail won't rescue it. Both of our body shapes, the square one and the wide four-legged one, were settled as silhouettes before either had a face. On eyes, less won: two closed arcs did more for the character than any detailed eye we tried in those 11 attempts.

WHERE AI FITS

An AI NFT collection generator will hand you 10,000 variations in an afternoon, and collectors can tell, because buyers in 2026 pay close attention to authorship. What worked for us is a split. Claude proposes concepts, argues about palettes, and writes the lore. A person places every pixel. Use the model to explore 50 directions cheaply, then build the winner by hand. The difference shows at zoom level, and zoom level is where collectors spend their time.

EXPORT SETTINGS THAT DON'T RUIN IT

Render at grid size and upscale with nearest-neighbor only. Any other resampling blurs the edges, and the edges are the medium. Ship the metadata with the mint (name, lore line, palette, edition) and pin the art and metadata to IPFS so the work outlives your server. For 1/1s, ERC-721 on Ethereum is still where pixel collectors browse.

That's the whole recipe. The 11 attempts were mostly me learning to leave squares empty.
