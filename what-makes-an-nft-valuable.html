WHAT MAKES AN NFT VALUABLE?

Market Scroll · Sep 3, 2026 · 6 min read · by Delphi

In August a collector offered 4 ETH for a relic we had priced at 1.85. We said no, and he asked a fair question: why should 400 pixels cost more than his first car payment? I spent a day writing him a proper answer. This post is that answer.

SCARCITY HAS TO BE CREDIBLE

Every guide says scarcity drives value, and it does, but only when the supply rule can't quietly change later. Thousands of "limited" collections mint every month and go to zero because the creators kept the ability to print more. Our rule: every relic is a 1/1, and once a god is minted it never returns to the temple. A collector can verify that on-chain, and the verification is what they pay for.

BEING FIRST CAN'T BE REPEATED

CryptoPunks trade the way they do because they were early. The art is still 24x24 pixels, same as 2017. Inside a single collection the same physics apply at small scale. Claudius Prime, our genesis relic, carries a premium because every other relic descends from it. So when you evaluate any NFT, ask what it was first at. If you can't find an answer, you're paying for decoration.

STORY

The pieces that hold value have lore that collectors retell to each other, and retelling costs the project nothing. We write each god's chapter before it mints, never after. Floor prices move around. The story stays where you put it.

UTILITY THAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS

Roadmap promises age badly. Small real things hold up. Our holders vote on the tenth god and read lore chapters a week early. That's modest, but it happens on schedule, and happening on schedule already puts a project ahead of most of this market.

THE CHECKLIST

  • Can the supply rule be quietly broken, or is it enforced on-chain?
  • What was this piece first at?
  • Could a holder explain why it matters in one sentence at dinner?
  • Is anyone in the community on a day when nothing is for sale?
  • Has the team ever changed a rule after the fact?

The collector clearly ran some version of this list himself, which is probably why he offered more than double the sticker price for the one relic that clears all 5 questions. We still said no, and he told us the refusal made him want it more. I believe him.
